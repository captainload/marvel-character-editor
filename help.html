🌟 1. System Overview & Architecture

Overview

The Marvel Super Heroes Character Editor is a dedicated tabletop RPG workstation engineered specifically for TSR's classic Marvel Super Heroes Roleplaying Game (Advanced and Basic sets) and the community-standard Classic Marvel Forever (CMF) rules modifications.

The application runs entirely within any modern web browser with zero external framework dependencies. All calculations, state transitions, dice rolls, and equipment catalogs operate 100% locally on your machine, ensuring full offline capability, zero latency, and persistent local storage.

Key Design Objective: Provide a unified digital workspace that honors the nostalgic 1980s TSR FASERIP rulebooks while equipping modern Game Masters and players with rapid point-buy budgets, automated Universal Action Table resolution, invention crafting, and clean printable character sheets.

💰 3. CMF Point-Buy Engine & Budget Accounting

Character Creation

The editor features an interactive, automated Classic Marvel Forever Point-Buy Engine. Rather than relying on swingy random dice rolls, players build heroes according to balanced Character Point (CP) budgets.

Standard CP Budget Tiers & Dynamic Adjustments

  • 300 CP — Costumed Adventurer / Low-Powered (Default): Costumed mystery men, neighborhood vigilantes, and young heroes (e.g., Daredevil, Moon Knight, Hawkeye).
  • 150 CP — Skilled Human / Agent: Highly trained non-powered agents, detectives, and athletic investigators.
  • 200 CP — Street Vigilante / Mystery Man: Hard-boiled street fighters, vigilantes, and low-powered heroes.
  • 400 CP — Established Hero / Mutant: Seasoned heroes, iconic mutants, and experienced crime-fighters (e.g., Spider-Man, Wolverine, Cyclops).
  • 500 CP — Major Superhero / Avenger: Frontline powerhouse team members and premier heroes (e.g., Iron Man, Storm, Colossus).
  • 600 CP — World-Class Hero / Powerhouse: Elite heavyweights, powerhouse champions, and high-tier heroes (e.g., Thor, Hulk, Silver Surfer).
  • Custom CP: Enter any custom budget for homebrew campaigns or custom scenarios.
  • Changeable Budget & Expenditure Guard: You can adjust your CP budget at any time during character creation via the CP Budget & Usage Breakdown modal. The engine strictly requires that your new budget cannot be lower than your current CP expenditure. If reducing the budget would put the hero over budget, you must reduce your CP expenditures (e.g. lowering ability ranks, or removing powers, talents, or contacts) before the lower budget can be applied.

CP Breakdown Pop-up & Starting CP Budget Slider (-25% to +50%)

The Character Point Breakdown dialog provides comprehensive accounting visibility into your character's resource spending:

  • Itemized Category Breakdown: See exact points spent across Abilities, Powers, Talents, Contacts, and Resources in a clear summary table with spent totals.
  • Visual Progress Meter: A real-time progress bar shows capacity utilization and turns warning-red if your build exceeds available CP.
  • Starting CP Budget Slider in Creation Wizard: The starting CP adjustment slider is located exclusively in the New Character Creation Setup dialog. Scale your starting CP budget from -25% up to +50% (in discrete increments of 25%: -25%, 0%, +25%, +50%) relative to your selected tier's base budget.
  • Resetting Starting Budget: To adjust or reset your character's starting budget after creation, start a new character or roll back to the first character save point in Revision History. The first save point occurs immediately after the hero name, CP allotment, and physical form are chosen.

New Character Generation Mode Prompt (TSR Standard, TSR Modeled, CMF Point-Buy)

When a new character is created (via File > New Character or on initial launch without a saved character), the application presents an interactive Generation Mode Selection dialog allowing players to choose their desired ruleset and creation method:

  • 🎲 TSR Standard (1986 Random Roll): Canonical 1986 Player's Book rolling rules. Roll Origin on UPB Table 1, FASERIP abilities on Table 1, power slots on Table 2, and talents/contacts using the interactive 3D dice generator. Rolled slot limits are strictly enforced. Characters can graduate to Modeled mode at any time.
  • 📐 TSR Modeled (Free-Form): Free-form TSR character modeling without random roll tables or slot constraints. Directly assign FASERIP abilities, superpower ranks, and traits. Trait costs are tracked in real-time in Karma Points (KP) with optional GM campaign budget limits.
  • ⚖️ CMF Point-Buy: Classic Marvel Forever point-buy system. Start with a Character Point budget (300 CP default tier), choose a starting Physical Form with attribute modifiers, and spend points to purchase abilities and powers. Opens the Character Creation Setup Wizard.

Character Creation Phase, KP Lock & Finalization Flow

To prevent exploits and uphold system balance, the editor enforces a strict separation between Character Creation and Session Play:

  • Karma Points (KP) Locked to R+I+P: During the Character Creation Phase, Karma steppers (-1, +1) and manual KP adjustment fields are completely disabled. Starting Karma strictly tracks derived Reason + Intuition + Psyche. Karma cannot be spent to purchase traits or artificially inflated before play begins.
  • Free Character Point (CP) Spending: Players freely allocate Character Points across primary abilities, superpowers, talents, contacts, and resources. Any changes legal under your selected form and tier are permitted in real-time. If total spending exceeds your CP budget, the interface issues clear warning alerts and turns meters red.
  • Finalize Character Creation Button & Persistent Topbar Throbber: Located in the persistent top toolbar (the pulsing Creating throbber / Finalize button), this button completes hero creation and permanently transitions your character into active Session Play from any tab. Finalization unlocks manual Karma adjustments, live Karma spending in the Universal Die Roller, and character advancement. Finalization requires that total CP spent does not exceed your assigned budget.
  • Optional House Rule — Convert Unspent CP to KP: Located in File > Preferences / House Rules, enabling this rule allows players who finalize their character with up to 5 leftover, unspent Character Points to convert those remaining points into bonus Starting Karma at a 1 CP to 5 KP rate (added directly to starting KP). When disabled, all Character Points must be spent (0 CP remaining) before a character can be finalized.
  • Rewinding Creation: To return to the Character Creation Phase at any time, open 📈 Progression and roll back to Revision #1 (Initial save point). This returns the character to creation mode, re-enables free CP configuration, and relocks KP until finalized anew.

TSR 1986 Random Generation & Vanilla Limitations (PB pp. 4–10)

In addition to modern CMF Point-Buy, the editor provides authentic, canonical support for the original 1986 Advanced Set Player's Book random character generation system:

  • Step 1: Physical Form & Origin (UPB Table 1 & Dragon #122): Roll d100 to determine physical form across 54 canonical forms and sub-forms (Normal Humans, Mutants, Altered Humans, High Humans, Demihumans, Cyborgs, Robots, Androids, Aliens, Energy Beings, Swarms, Deities). Sets the governing Table 1 column (Cols 1–5), physical/mental ability shifts (+1CS, +2CS), bonus talents/contacts, and power slots bounds.
  • Step 2: Primary Abilities (PB p. 6, Table 1): Roll all seven FASERIP abilities on Table 1 according to the Physical Form's column, automatically applying form ability column shifts (+1CS/+2CS). Individual abilities can be re-rolled if allowed by campaign house rules. Resources are rolled on Table 1 or set to standard.
  • Step 3: Superpowers (PB p. 10 Category Model): Roll the number of power slots on Table 2, then roll each power's Category on Table 4 (Defensive, Detection, Fighting, Travel, Matter Control, Energy Emission, Self-Alteration, Matter Conversion, Mental Enhancement, or Magical). Following the Player's Book page 10 example, the player chooses their specific power from that rolled category, then rolls its starting rank on Table 1.
  • Vanilla Power Limitations (PB p. 9): In original TSR rules, accepting a limitation grants a +1CS (one column shift higher) rank bump to the power. Each limitation tier has a strict maximum ceiling (Maximum of Excellent, Remarkable, Incredible, Amazing, Monstrous, or Unearthly). A limitation can only raise a power rank up to that ceiling tier (e.g., a Remarkable ceiling can raise Good to Remarkable, but cannot raise a power that is already Remarkable or higher). If a power rank is already at or above the ceiling, the limitation cannot be applied.
  • Step 4: Talents & Contacts (PB pp. 7 & 10 & UPB): Roll talent slots on Table 3 (0 to 4 slots + form bonus). Select from all 56 canonical talents across all 7 disciplines (Weapon, Fighting, Professional, Scientific, Mystic/Mental, Piloting, Other) with interactive category filtering. Establish contacts across 19 canonical categories or add custom NPC contacts.
  • Unified 3-Way Mode Switch & Combined Pulsing Header Tag: Character creation strictly distinguishes between TSR standard (canonical 1986 random roll), TSR modeled (free-form modeling with Karma Point tracking), and CMF CP buy (Classic Marvel Forever point-buy). In the creation block on the Main Stats tab, these three modes are unified into an intuitive three-way switch where the active mode is brightly highlighted and clicking any segment seamlessly switches modes. In the top toolbar, the previous "Creating" and mode tags are combined into a single, pulsing status badge (displaying TSR standard, TSR modeled, or CMF CP buy) with theme-coordinated glow effects that stays visible throughout character creation and transitions to "Finalize" once all creation requirements are satisfied.
  • Interactive TSR Submode Selection Dialog: Switching to TSR mode does not immediately open the random generator. Instead, an interactive selection modal appears presenting clear cards for both Random Roll Mode and Modeled Mode with explanations:
    • Choosing Random Roll Mode sets the character to TSR Random mode and launches the TSR Generator modal with 3D dice.
    • Choosing Modeled Mode sets the character to TSR Modeled mode and directly opens the character sheet without launching the generator, allowing free-form modeling immediately.
    • Users are reminded that they can switch from Random to Modeled mode at any time, but returning from Modeled to Random requires rolling back to Revision #1 in the Progression tab.
  • TSR Modeled Mode & Submode Split Toggle: When in TSR mode, the creation block displays an obvious submode split toggle between Random and Modeled:
    • Random Mode (Default): Pure canonical 1986 Advanced Set Player's Book rolling rules with strict rolled slot limits across powers, talents, and contacts.
    • Modeled Mode (Free-Form): Bypasses rolled slot limits and random roll requirements, allowing GMs and players to model established heroes or free-form concepts. Trait investment is tracked in real-time in Karma Points (KP) using canonical advancement scaling (Abilities: 10 KP per rank number; Powers: 100/200 KP base + 10/20 per rank number minus limitation discounts; Talents: 100/200 KP; Contacts: 50 KP each; Resources: 10 KP per rank number).
    • Campaign KP Budget Limit: In Modeled mode, GMs can optionally set a maximum campaign KP ceiling in the Mode Detail dialog, displaying live budget tracking (e.g. 2,450 / 3,000 KP (550 left)) and warning when over budget.
    • Manual Trait Assignment Protection: If a user in TSR Random mode begins manually assigning or adjusting traits directly on the sheet (such as FASERIP ability ranks, Resources ranks, adding powers, changing power ranks on power cards, or adding talents/contacts), the application displays a confirmation prompt. The prompt warns that manual assignment switches the character to TSR Modeled mode (bypassing slot limits and switching to KP tracking) and that reverting back to Random mode will require a history rollback. If the user cancels, the manual edit is aborted and the sheet remains in TSR Random mode.
    • Irreversible Activation Warning: Because Modeled mode permits free-form creation without slot constraints, entering Modeled mode displays an explicit warning modal confirming that switching to Modeled mode is irreversible without rolling back character edit history to Revision #1 (or before Modeled mode was activated) on the Progression tab. Attempting to toggle back to Random from Modeled informs the user and offers direct navigation to the Progression tab.
  • Multi-Window Pop-out & Cheat Sheet Reference: The TSR 1986 Character Generator features an independent ↗ Pop-out window mode, allowing you to detach the rolling workshop into a separate floating monitor window with preserved dimensions, live theme synchronization, and full interactive control. Additionally, click 📖 Cheat Sheet directly in the generator header to open the Rules Cheat Sheet side-by-side during character rolling.

🎲 5. Universal Action Table Roller & FEAT Resolution

Dice Roller

The integrated Universal Action Table Dice Roller automates FEAT resolution according to official TSR/CMF rules. You can trigger rolls from any ability card, combat maneuver row, or open the roller independently.

  • d100 Engine: True random roll generation with animated visual feedback.
  • Dynamic Color Band Matching: Instantly evaluates the roll against the active rank column:
    • Dark Blue: Fumble / Blunder / Weapon Mishap (CMF mode).
    • White: Failure.
    • Green: Standard Success.
    • Yellow: Remarkable Success (special effects trigger).
    • Red: Spectacular Success (maximum impact / critical triumph).
  • Column Shift Steppers: Apply temporary buffs or penalties (-3 to +3 CS and custom) with real-time recalculation of percentile thresholds.
  • Karma Spending Stepper: Spend available character Karma point-by-point to adjust the roll upward into the next color band!
  • Pop-out Multi-Window Mode: Click the ↗ Pop Out button to detach the roller into an independent browser window for dual-monitor or tablet tabletop setups.

Power Limitations & Restrictions Pop-up

Under both canonical TSR rulebooks and modern CMF Point-Buy design, superpowers can have Limitations and Restrictions that temper their scope, reflect narrative vulnerabilities, and provide Character Point (CP) discounts:

  • Interactive Limitation Modal (⚖️ Limitations): Every power card features a ⚖️ Limitations action in its card menu dropdown (and a clickable limitation tag in the card header when active). Clicking this opens the Power Limitation & Restrictions pop-up dialog.
  • Mandatory Limitations (Automatic Interception): Certain canonical powers are required by official rules to possess limitations to maintain game balance and Judge narrative control. These include Precognition [M24], Alter Ego [S1], Induced Berserking, Energy Absorption [DT1], and Split Form / Duplication [S23]. When one of these powers is added to your hero, the Limitation Modal appears automatically in mandatory mode. The power cannot be added to the character sheet until a valid mandatory limitation is selected.
  • Provided Canonical Limitations vs. Custom Limitations: The dialog offers a curated library of canonical limitations with pre-configured default Intensity ranks and CP discounts (e.g., Uncontrollable / Emotionally Triggered, Physical Contact Required, External Focus / Reversible Only, Berserk Psyche FEAT, Vulnerability to Specific Energy). Alternatively, players can define Custom Limitations with user-defined names, detailed descriptions, custom Intensity ranks (from Feeble to Shift Z), and CP discount values (-5 to -20 CP).
  • CP Discounts & Minimum Cost Floor: Limitations provide a discount against the power's final Character Point cost (typically -10 CP for standard limitations, up to -20 CP for severe constraints). Under CMF point-buy balance guidelines, a power cannot be discounted below a minimum floor of 5 CP.
TSR Canonical Rules vs. CMF Point-Buy Limitations Explained:

1. Canonical TSR Rules (Ultimate Powers Book & Dragon Magazine #122 / #134):
In original TSR rules, limitations were primarily narrative balancing mechanisms and GM-arbitrated intensity checks.
  • Mandatory Narrative Constraints: High-reality-warping powers like Precognition were strictly forbidden from functioning as on-demand omniscience. The Judge dictated when visions struck (often through emotional trauma, physical contact, or dreams), or required Psyche FEAT rolls against specific intensities to perceive fragments of the future.
  • Voluntary Limitations & Column Shifts: When players voluntarily placed severe limitations on a standard power (e.g., Cyclops' ruby quartz visor requirement, or Thor's 60-second separation from Mjolnir reverting him to mortal form), the GM rewarded them with positive column shifts (usually +1CS or +2CS bonus to the power rank) or lowered intensity requirements when the power did function.
  • Limitation Ranks: In TSR rules, the rank attached to a limitation represents the Intensity Rank of the FEAT required to resist, trigger, or operate the power (e.g., a Remarkable (30) Psyche FEAT to pull out of an induced berserk state, or a Typical (6) Intensity sensory trigger).
2. Classic Marvel Forever (CMF) Point-Buy Rules:
In the CMF Point-Buy system, power limitations are quantified mathematically into Character Point (CP) Cost Reductions:
  • Rather than granting variable column shifts, limitations reduce the point-buy cost by 5 to 20 CP (with 10 CP as standard baseline).
  • This allows heroes with restricted or conditional powers (like Human Torch needing oxygen/heat to ignite, or Rogue unable to touch without draining) to be built accurately within street-level and mid-tier CP budgets without sacrificing core ability ranks.

TSR Mode Power Slot Limits, Starred Powers & Bonus Slots

In authentic TSR 1986 Random Generation mode, superhero power acquisition is strictly governed by the number of power slots rolled on Table 2, canonical slot costs, and slot-granting powers:

  • Strict Power Slot Enforcement: In TSR mode, the power builder limits your character to the exact number of superpower slots rolled during creation. The live slot tally is displayed in the tab badge (Slots: X / Y (Z powers)) and in the top creation mode strip.
  • Starred (*) Heavyweight Powers (2 Slots): Exceptional, heavyweight powers marked with an asterisk (e.g., Alter Ego, Energy Mastery, Power Simulation, Magic Control) consume 2 power slots per canonical TSR rules (Player's Book p. 19 & UPB pp. 16–19). The builder automatically debits two slots when a starred power is added, and prevents adding a two-slot power if only one slot remains.
  • Bonus Power Slot Grants (+1 Slot): Canonical powers from the Ultimate Powers Book that grant an additional power slot upon generation are fully supported:
    • PC5 Energy Source: Grants +1 additional power slot ("If you roll this Power, go ahead and roll an additional Power.").
    • MG2 Energy Source (Magic): Mystical equivalent granting +1 additional power slot.
    • PC7 Focus: Grants +1 additional power slot ("Roll again for an additional Power.").
    • PC12 Residual Absorption: Grants +1 additional power slot ("When the player generates this Power, he gets to generate an additional Power as well.").
    When any of these powers are added to the character, the hero's maximum power slot ceiling dynamically expands (e.g., 4 slot(s) total (3 rolled + 1 bonus)), regardless of whether the power is chosen first, middle, or last.
  • Full Slot Protection & Tooltips: Once all allocated slots are filled, the + Add Power to Sheet button is disabled with an explanatory tooltip (Power slots full: X/Y slots allocated). The character creation finalizer verifies that all rolled slots are allocated before hero generation can be completed.

🔬 7. Machines of Doom — Advanced Invention Lab

Invention Lab

The Invention Lab Tab implements the official TSR Advanced Set Machines of Doom invention rules with complete fidelity. Creators can design custom gadgets, mystical artifacts, vehicles, armor, or reverse-engineer rulebook equipment:

The 3-Phase Invention Workflow

  1. Phase 1: Blueprint Design FEAT — The inventor rolls Reason (plus applicable scientific/mystical talents) against the project's target complexity rank. Success drafts a functional blueprint!
  2. Phase 2: Resource Procurement FEAT — The creator rolls Resources against the raw material rank. Success acquires all necessary exotic alloys, arcane reagents, and microcircuits.
  3. Phase 3: Workshop Assembly FEAT — The creator rolls Reason in their workshop over the calculated build duration (in days/weeks). Success produces the completed device!

Invention Source Types: Tech vs. Magic

Toggle between Tech Invention and Magic / Mystical Artifact. The interface dynamically updates terminology, hardware boosts, resource components, and category options:

  • Tech Devices: Weapons, Armor, Vehicles, Sensors, Exoskeletons, and Consumable (gadget, ammo, etc.).
  • Magic Inventions: Talismans, Amulets, Enchanted Weapons, Mystic Armor, and Consumable (rune, potion, etc).

Hardware Boosts & Limitations

Customize your device with over 10 mechanical modifiers:
• Boosts: Expanded Capability (+1 Power), Overload Surge, Remote Control, Concealed Chassis.
• Limitations: Fragile Chassis, Fuel/Power Dependency, Burnout Chance, Feedback Jolt, Finite Charges.

Reverse-Engineering Rulebook Equipment

Select any prebuilt equipment from the official rulebooks in Section 2. The engine instantly computes its raw material procurement intensity, blueprint design difficulty, and workshop build time. With one click, you can reverse-engineer it directly into your Mastered Schematics Archive!

Mastered Schematics Benefit: Any device previously built or successfully reverse-engineered is stored in your character's Known Blueprints Archive. Building from a known blueprint automatically bypasses the Phase 1 Blueprint Design FEAT!

🥋 9. Talents, Contacts & Character Advancement

Talents & Contacts

Heroes are defined as much by their mortal skills and relationships as their superpowers:

  • Talents Catalog: Organized by category (Fighting Masteries, Martial Arts, Weapon Skills, Professional / Vocational, Scientific Specialties, Occult, and Other).
  • Inline Specialization: For talents with choices (e.g., Weapons Specialist or Scientific Specialty), enter your custom focus directly inline.

💡 Waterfox & Gecko-Based Browsers — Opening as Standalone Windows:

Unlike standard Firefox (which defaults to opening sized pop-up windows in separate OS windows), Waterfox often diverts all pop-ups into browser tabs. If pop-outs open as tabs in Waterfox, check the following two preferences in about:config:

  1. Type about:config into your Waterfox address bar and press Enter (click Accept the Risk and Continue if prompted).
  2. Search for: browser.link.open_newwindow — set its value to 2 (forces links that target new windows to open in a separate window instead of a new tab).
  3. Search for: browser.link.open_newwindow.restriction — ensure its value is 2 (standard mode: allows scripted pop-ups with custom sizes to open in separate windows).
  4. Alternatively, in Waterfox Settings ➔ General ➔ Tabs, ensure "Open links in tabs instead of new windows" is unchecked, and verify any third-party tab extensions (such as Tab Mix Plus) allow window pop-outs.
