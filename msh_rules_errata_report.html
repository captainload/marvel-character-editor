#01. Origin Rank Generation Column Shift Mismatch Player's Book p. 6 & UPB Table 1
Mechanical Contradiction
Problem Passage:
"Mutant: Roll on Column 1 for all abilities. Roll once on the Power Category table... +1CS to Endurance." (p. 6)
"Normal Human: Roll on Column 2 for all abilities..." (p. 6)
Analysis of the Error:
In the 1986 Advanced Set, Table 1 on page 5 expanded to five columns. Column 1 yields lower rank bands (e.g. Feeble/Poor), while Column 2 provides the standard heroic distribution. The Player's Book text erroneously copy-pasted the 1984 Basic Set rule (where Column 1 was the hero column), making mutants statistically inferior to baseline humans prior to power rolls!
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Standardized in UPB Table 1 and CMF rules: Mutants roll on Column 2 (Column 4 in high-powered campaigns) and apply their +1CS Endurance bonus. Normal humans roll on Column 1 or 2 with their standard compensation of additional talents and resources.
#02. Health Calculation: "Rolled Values" vs Fixed Rank Numbers Player's Book p. 7 & 1991 Revised Set p. 6
Editorial Glitch
Problem Passage:
"Health is the sum of the initial rolled values of Fighting, Agility, Strength, and Endurance." (p. 7)
Analysis of the Error:
The phrasing "initial rolled values" creates confusion between the percentile d100 roll (e.g. 01–100) and the numerical score of the resulting rank band (e.g. Remarkable = 30).
Latest Authoritative Fix:
The 1991 Revised Set explicitly clarifies: "Health is the sum of the standard numerical values of Fighting, Agility, Strength, and Endurance (Health = F + A + S + E)."
#03. Secret Identity Starting Popularity Discrepancy Player's Book pp. 7–8 & Judge's Book p. 9
Mechanical Contradiction
Problem Passage:
Page 7: "Secret ID characters have two Popularity scores: one for their hero identity and 0 for their secret identity."
Page 8: "A secret identity has a normal human popularity (usually 10)..."
Analysis of the Error:
In FASERIP, 0 Popularity means Shift 0 (universally despised, feared, or unknown). Peter Parker or Matt Murdock are respected citizens, not hated pariahs. Page 7's "0" was an editorial draft artifact meant to convey "not modified by superhero exploits."
Latest Authoritative Fix:
The Judge's Book and 1991 rules confirm: Civilian Secret IDs start at Popularity 10 (or modified by profession). Superhero persona Popularity is tracked independently on its own track.
#04. Number of Powers Roll Ceiling vs Maximum Slots Player's Book p. 9 & UPB pp. 11–13
Vague Omission
Problem Passage:
"Roll on the Number of Powers Table (01–15: 1; 16–45: 2; 46–75: 3; 76–90: 4; 91–98: 5; 99–00: 6). A hero may have up to 8 powers through bonuses..." (p. 9)
Analysis of the Error:
The Player's Book provided no table entries or clear rules for how the 7th and 8th powers were rolled.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Superseded by the UPB Physical Form Generation System: The number of powers is determined by the character's physical form (Normal Human, Mutant, Android, Energy Being, etc.), with custom roll tables yielding up to 16 slots for high-tier entity forms.
#05. Talent Slots: Table Roll vs Reason Bonus Stacking Player's Book p. 11 & CMF Talents Book
Editorial Clarification
Problem Passage:
"Roll on the Number of Talents Table (01–30: 0; 31–60: 1; 61–85: 2; 86–95: 3; 96–00: 4). Heroes gain +1 talent for each Reason rank above Typical." (p. 11)
Analysis of the Error:
High Reason heroes could end up with 8+ talents while low Reason heroes could have 0, creating extreme skill disparity.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Consolidated standard: Base talents are rolled on the table (or set by physical form) +1 bonus talent per Reason rank band above Typical (Good +1, Excellent +2, Remarkable +3, Incredible +4). All player characters receive a minimum of 1 talent representing their core civilian background.
#06. Altered Human Origin Stat Rolling Conflict Player's Book p. 6 vs UPB p. 10
Mechanical Contradiction
Problem Passage:
Player's Book p. 6: "Altered Human: Roll on Column 1 for all abilities... +1CS to any one ability."
UPB p. 10: "Altered Humans roll on Column 2 for all abilities, +1CS to one physical ability."
Analysis of the Error:
The Player's Book assigned Altered Humans (like Spider-Man or the Fantastic Four) to Column 1, giving them subpar starting abilities before their accidental alteration.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
The UPB supersedes the Player's Book: Altered Humans roll on Column 2 and apply +1CS to one physical ability (Fighting, Agility, Strength, or Endurance).
#07. Starting Resource Rank Determination Ambiguity Player's Book p. 7 & 1991 Revised p. 8
Vague Table Reference
Problem Passage:
"Roll 1d100 on Column 1 of the Random Ranks Table to determine Resources." (p. 7)
Analysis of the Error:
Rolling on Column 1 produced a 65% probability of Feeble or Poor resources, meaning nearly all superheroes were homeless or impoverished by default.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
1991 Revised Box and UPB rules: Characters roll on Column 2 for Resources or take a default rank based on their civilian occupation (e.g., Student = Poor, Professional = Typical, Corporate/Hero for Hire = Good, Wealthy = Remarkable+).
#08. Karma Pool: Adventure Reset vs Persistent Bank Player's Book p. 7 & Judge's Book p. 12
Mechanical Ambiguity
Problem Passage:
"Karma is the sum of Reason, Intuition, and Psyche." (p. 7)
"Karma is earned and spent... If Karma drops to 0, heroes cannot affect die rolls." (p. 35)
Analysis of the Error:
The Player's Book failed to state whether $Karma = R + I + P$ is a one-time starting balance, or if characters refresh back to this baseline at the start of a new adventure.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Official Judge's Book clarification: Starting Karma ($R + I + P$) is a persistent pool that carries over between sessions. However, at the beginning of a major new story arc/issue, a hero whose Karma has dropped below their baseline ($R+I+P$) replenishes back to their natural $R+I+P$ value.
#09. Martial Arts B vs Martial Arts C Grappling Typo Player's Book p. 12 & p. 27
Authoritative Typo Correction
Problem Passage:
Page 12 (Talents): "Martial Arts B: Gives a +1CS to Fighting when engaged in unarmed combat, and +1CS to grappling."
Page 27 (Grappling): "Martial Arts C gives +1CS to Strength for Grappling FEATs and damage."
Analysis of the Error:
Direct copy-paste inversion between the talent catalog on p. 12 and the combat resolution chapter on p. 27.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Standardized in all later Handbooks and CMF Talents Book:
• Martial Arts B: +1CS to Fighting in unarmed Hand-to-Hand slugfest.
• Martial Arts C: +1CS to Strength for Grappling FEATs, hold damage, and escapes.
#10. Dodging Failure Penalty (+1CS to Attacker) Reversal Player's Book p. 24 vs 1991 Revised Box p. 14
Officially Reversed Rule
Problem Passage:
"If the dodging FEAT is a White result, the hero fails to dodge, and incoming ranged attacks receive a +1CS to hit." (p. 24)
Analysis of the Error:
Punishing a defender with a +1CS penalty to enemy attacks on a failed dodge made attempting to dodge a statistically irrational choice compared to standing still.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Officially updated in the 1991 Revised Set (TSR 6899): A White Dodge roll is simply a failure—it applies 0CS penalty to the attacker, but does not grant the attacker a bonus (+0CS).
#11. Charging Damage Cap & Self-Inflicted Recoil Player's Book p. 25 vs 1991 Revised p. 15
Officially Capped Rule
Problem Passage:
"Damage from a Charge is based on Endurance rank or Land Speed, plus +1CS for each area moved..." (p. 25)
Analysis of the Error:
Without a cap, high-speed characters moving 10–20 areas could generate Shift X/Y/Z damage with no recoil risk.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
1991 Revised Rules: Charging bonus is capped at +3CS maximum. Furthermore, the charging character suffers recoil damage equal to the target's Material Strength or Body Armor (unless protected by Body Armor/Invulnerability).
#12. Slam Collision Barrier Damage Clarification Player's Book p. 26 & Judge's Book p. 18
Authoritative Ruling
Problem Passage:
"...the character takes damage equal to the Material Strength of the barrier or the Strength of the attacker..." (p. 26)
Analysis of the Error:
Ambiguity regarding "or the Strength of the attacker" could cause a weak punch to inflict massive barrier-level damage upon slamming into a sturdy vault.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Standardized in the Judge's Book: Collision damage equals the lesser of the barrier's Material Strength or the attacker's Strength (capped by the kinetic energy delivered).
#13. Multiple Attacks vs Same-Area Maneuvering Player's Book p. 30 & Judge's Book p. 20
Mechanical Contradiction
Problem Passage:
"A hero making multiple attacks cannot move in that round." (p. 30)
Analysis of the Error:
Stating that a hero "cannot move" froze martial artists in place, preventing them from stepping 3 feet to strike an adjacent foe in the same area.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Judge's Book clarification: A character making multiple attacks cannot perform area-to-area movement (0 areas moved), but may freely pivot, adjust, and strike targets within the same area.
#14. Wolverine Claws Damage Reconciliation Player's Book p. 13 & Gamer's Handbooks Vols 1–8
Authoritative Stat Fix
Problem Passage:
Player's Book p. 13: Claws deal Good (10) damage.
Advanced Rules Roster & Handbooks: Claws deal Excellent (20) damage.
Analysis of the Error:
Wolverine has Remarkable (30) Strength. Under weapon rules (p. 27), a weapon inflicts either weapon damage or the hero's Strength, whichever is higher. If claws were Good (10), his bare fist would do more damage than adamantium claws!
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Standardized in all official Gamer's Handbooks (1988–1992): Adamantium claws deal Excellent (20) base Edged damage or can channel his Remarkable (30) Strength, ignoring Body Armor up to Monstrous (75).
#15. Armor-Piercing Ammo Resolution Order Player's Book p. 28 & MA8 Weapons Locker p. 4
Authoritative Sequence Fix
Problem Passage:
"Armor-Piercing ammunition reduces target's Body Armor by 2CS." (p. 62)
Analysis of the Error:
The combat damage resolution sequence in the Player's Book did not specify whether the -2CS reduction was temporary for that attack or permanently destroyed the target's armor.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Codified in MA8 Weapons Locker: The -2CS armor reduction applies only for that specific bullet attack. It does not permanently degrade the armor unless a structural break FEAT is achieved.
#16. Evading Hand-to-Hand Attack Difficulty Player's Book p. 24 & 1991 Revised Box p. 15
Clarified Mechanic
Problem Passage:
"Evading is used against Slugfest attacks... the hero makes an Agility FEAT." (p. 24)
Analysis of the Error:
The original text didn't clarify whether the FEAT roll was static or opposed by the attacker's Fighting rank.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
1991 Revised Rules: Evading replaces all actions for that turn. The hero makes a standard Agility FEAT: Green confers -2CS to incoming Slugfest attacks, Yellow confers -4CS, Red confers -6CS.
#17. Initiative: Team Roll vs Individual Talent Modifiers Player's Book p. 22 & Judge's Book p. 15
Authoritative Procedure
Problem Passage:
"Each side rolls 1d10 for initiative... Martial Arts E gives +1 to initiative." (p. 12, 22)
Analysis of the Error:
Because initiative is rolled per team, the rules were unclear whether Martial Arts E (+1CS) modified the whole team's die roll or allowed that individual hero to act first.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Judge's Book & 1991 Rules: The team rolls 1d10. The side with the highest individual modifier (e.g. Martial Arts E, Lightning Reflexes, Intuition rank) adds their modifier to the team initiative roll.
#18. Regeneration Recovery Rate Typo (Hourly vs Round) Player's Book p. 55 & UPB p. 23
Famous Historical Typo
Problem Passage:
"The hero recovers his power rank in Health points every hour..." (p. 55)
Example in same paragraph: "Wolverine recovers 30 points of Health every turn (6 seconds)!"
Analysis of the Error:
A famous typographical blunder in TSR history. The text says "every hour," directly contradicting the example which correctly states "every turn."
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Standard 3-tier healing hierarchy (UPB and 1991 Rules):
1. Natural Endurance Healing: Recovers Endurance rank number per day (24 hours).
2. Recovery Power: Recovers Endurance rank number per hour.
3. Regeneration Power: Recovers Power Rank number per turn / round (6 seconds).
#19. Force Field & Body Armor Sequential Absorption Player's Book pp. 47–48 & Gamer's Handbooks
Authoritative Stacking Rule
Problem Passage:
Entries for Body Armor (p. 47) and Force Field (p. 48) failed to state how they interact when possessed by the same character (e.g. Iron Man or Magneto).
Analysis of the Error:
Adding rank numbers together breaks game balance (e.g. Remarkable 30 + Incredible 40 = Monstrous 70).
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Officially clarified across all Gamer's Handbooks: Defenses do not add together. Force Fields absorb damage first. Any damage that penetrates the Force Field is subsequently reduced by Body Armor.
#20. Movement Conversion Constant (1 Area/Turn = 15 MPH) Player's Book p. 19 & p. 59
Mathematical Discrepancy Fix
Problem Passage:
Table on page 19: 1 area/turn = 15 mph.
Flight on page 59 has rounded areas that do not match the exact 15 mph multiplier at intermediate ranks.
Analysis of the Error:
Because 1 turn = 6 seconds and 1 area = 132 feet (44 yards), 1 area in 6 seconds is exactly 22 feet/sec = 15 miles per hour. Handbooks rounded areas arbitrarily, causing discrepancies.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Standardized in all builder calculations: 1 Area per Turn = exactly 15 Miles Per Hour (or 22 ft/second).
#21. Hyper-Speed vs Hyper-Running Interaction Rules Dragon Magazine #151 p. 92 (Son of the Ultimate Addenda)
Official Author Ruling
Problem Passage:
Players assumed Hyper-Speed (multiple actions per turn) multiplied Hyper-Running (ground speed), allowing characters to travel at thousands of areas per round.
Analysis of the Error:
Speed multiplication caused game-breaking movement and punch velocity exploits.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Codified by David Martin in Dragon #151: Hyper-Running sets the maximum distance a hero can run in a round. Hyper-Speed allows the hero to perform multiple actions while traversing that distance, but does NOT multiply the total areas traveled.
#22. Power Stunts Karma Forfeiture & Mastery Success Count Player's Book p. 10 vs Judge's Book p. 24
Authoritative Reconciled Rule
Problem Passage:
Player's Book p. 10: Stunts cost 100 Karma and require 10 successful attempts for mastery.
Judge's Book p. 24: Requires 3 successful attempts for mastery.
Analysis of the Error:
Discrepancy between 10 attempts vs 3 attempts, and unclear whether the 100 Karma was spent if the FEAT failed.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Standard tournament & campaign consensus: 100 Karma is spent as an ante upon declaring the attempt. The modern consensus adopts the Judge's Book standard of 3 successful recorded attempts (or 1,000 Karma in advancement) to establish permanent mastery.
#23. Telepathy Range Progression Re-scaling Player's Book p. 54 vs UPB Range Tables
Smoothed Range Progression
Problem Passage:
Incredible: 50 areas (1.25 miles)... Amazing: Planetary (12,000 miles). (p. 54)
Analysis of the Error:
A four-orders-of-magnitude jump between Incredible and Amazing ranks broke all narrative progression.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Replaced by UPB Mental Range benchmarks: Amazing = 100 miles (regional); Monstrous = 3,000 miles (continental); Unearthly = 25,000 miles (planetary).
#24. Renamed Power: "Topological Change" to Elongation & Plasticity UPB MA3 & Dragon #122 p. 82
Renamed Reference Resolved
Problem Passage:
Manuscript references to "Topological Change" appeared in notes, but neither it nor Elongation appeared in the printed UPB.
Analysis of the Error:
Author David Martin explained in Dragon #122 that "Topological Change" was renamed during development to Elongation (S24) and Plasticity (S29), but due to a layout mix-up, both were omitted from the printed book!
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Codified as two distinct powers: S24 Elongation (stretching limbs/body) and S29 Plasticity (molding shape and malleability), completely retiring the draft term "Topological Change."
#25. Renamed Power: "Super-Breath" to Hyper Intake/Expulsion (P18) UPB & Dragon Magazine #134 p. 89
Renamed Reference Resolved
Problem Passage:
Mentioned casually in UPB power descriptions as "Super-Breath", but missing from the power tables.
Analysis of the Error:
David Martin intended Super-Breath to be an independent physical power, but it was left out of the UPB until fans petitioned for it.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Officially designated in Dragon #134 as P18 Hyper Intake/Expulsion (Super-Breath) under Physical Powers. Both names are aliased in our builder search.
#26. Renamed Power: "Super-Strength" to Hyper-Strength (P20) UPB & Dragon Magazine #134 p. 90
Renamed Reference Resolved
Problem Passage:
UPB powers frequently state "Requires Super-Strength," but no power named Super-Strength was on the UPB power tables.
Analysis of the Error:
In Dragon #134, the author noted: "I kept referring to this as a power, too, so here it is: P20 Hyper-Strength."
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Formally codified as P20 Hyper-Strength (allowing temporary "Hulk out" rank boosts or permanent strength expansion). Whenever older text says "Super-Strength," it refers to either P20 or a physical Strength rank of Remarkable (30) or higher.
#27. Renamed Talent: Tumbling vs Acrobatics Discrepancy Player's Book p. 12 & Character Folios
Renamed Reference Resolved
Problem Passage:
Player's Book p. 12 lists "Tumbling", but character sheets for Spider-Man, Daredevil, and Nightcrawler list "Acrobatics" (which was absent from the Player's Book talent table).
Analysis of the Error:
"Acrobatics" was carried over from the 1984 Basic Set into character sheets, while the Advanced Set author renamed it "Tumbling" and restricted its benefits.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Reconciled in CMF Ultimate Talents: Both talents exist distinctly:
• Acrobatics: +1CS to Agility for Dodging, Evading, and aerial maneuvers.
• Tumbling: +1CS to Agility for safe landing, reducing falling damage, and escaping holds.
#28. Renamed Talent: Weapon Specialist vs Weapons Master Player's Book p. 12 & MA8 Weapons Locker
Renamed Reference Resolved
Problem Passage:
Player's Book uses "Weapon Skills", "Weapons Specialist", and "Weapons Master" interchangeably with conflicting stacking rules.
Analysis of the Error:
Unclear whether a Weapon Specialist (+2CS) stacks with a category weapon skill (+1CS).
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Standardized in MA8 Weapons Locker:
• Weapon Group Skill: +1CS to hit with a specific category (Blunt, Sharp, Thrown, Guns, Bows).
• Weapon Specialist: +2CS to hit, +1 to initiative with ONE specific designated weapon (e.g. Captain America's Shield, Hawkeye's Bow). Does NOT stack with Group Skill.
• Weapons Master: +1CS to hit with all weapons.
#29. Renamed Power: Invulnerability vs True Invulnerability (D17) Player's Book p. 48 & UPB p. 25
Renamed Reference Resolved
Problem Passage:
Player's Book calls it "Invulnerability", whereas the UPB names it "True Invulnerability (D17)" and differentiates it from standard Body Armor.
Analysis of the Error:
Calling both powers "Invulnerability" caused players to confuse single-damage immunities (e.g. Fire Invulnerability) with universal damage resistance.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Adopt UPB terminology: Universal full-spectrum defense is D17 True Invulnerability. Specific resistances are termed Resistance to [Attack Type].
#30. Teleportation Range & Fatigue Rules Harmonization Player's Book p. 57 & UPB p. 64 (T20)
Authoritative Range Scale
Problem Passage:
Player's Book p. 57 states teleportation requires an "Endurance FEAT to avoid fatigue," but provides no table of fatigue consequences.
Analysis of the Error:
Missing fatigue consequences left Judges with no rules for characters like Nightcrawler making multiple rapid warps.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
UPB T20 rules: Teleporting within the power rank distance requires no roll. Teleporting with passengers or attempting blind jumps into unmapped areas requires a Green Endurance FEAT; failure results in immediate unconsciousness for 1–10 turns.
#31. Renamed Power: Mental Blast vs Psionic Attack (EC10) Player's Book p. 54 & UPB p. 30
Renamed Reference Resolved
Problem Passage:
Comics and folios cite "Mental Blast" (e.g. Psylocke), while the Player's Book treated it as a Telepathy power stunt, and UPB codified it under Energy Control.
Analysis of the Error:
Fragmented rules caused confusion over whether a character needed full Telepathy to project offensive mental daggers or bolts.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Standardized under EC10 Psionic Attack (Mental Blast): Can be possessed as an independent attack power or acquired as an optional power/stunt from Telepathy.
#32. Karma Spending: Pre-Roll vs Post-Roll Reconciliation Player's Book p. 35 vs p. 37 & 1991 Revised Box
Authoritative Reconciled Rule
Problem Passage:
Page 35: "Karma must be declared before rolling the dice."
Page 37: "A player may spend Karma to turn a failed roll into a success after the roll by adding Karma points equal to the difference."
Analysis of the Error:
Blatant chapter contradiction. The pre-roll restriction made players waste Karma on rolls that naturally succeeded.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Formally settled in the 1991 Revised Set and standard tournament rules: Players are permitted to spend Karma point-for-point after the dice are rolled to modify the result into Green, Yellow, or Red success, except when the natural roll is a catastrophic failure (01 on red FEATs).
#33. Advancement Cost: New Rank Number vs Difference Player's Book p. 42 & CMF Advancement Rules
Vague Cost Formula
Problem Passage:
"To increase an ability or power, the hero must spend Karma equal to 10 times the rank number of the new rank." (p. 42)
Analysis of the Error:
Paying the full new rank (e.g. 400 Karma for Incredible) discards prior investment, discouraging long-term campaign character progression.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Configurable in Builder: We provide both options in the advancement tracker:
• Strict RAW: Cost = 10 × New Rank Number.
• CMF Progressive Standard: Cost = 10 × (New Rank Number − Old Rank Number) + minimum 1 month game-time training.
#34. Death Effects on Superheroes: Stabilization & Revival Dragon Magazine #150 pp. 88–91 (Death Effects)
Authoritative Official Expansion
Problem Passage:
Player's Book p. 29 simply states a hero at 0 Health with shifting Endurance is "dying", but gave vague stabilization thresholds.
Analysis of the Error:
Lack of exact per-round degradation rates led to inconsistent Judge rulings on resuscitation and medical FEATs.
Latest Authoritative Fix:
Codified by David Martin in Dragon #150: A character at 0 Health loses 1 point of Endurance rank number per turn. A Green First Aid/Medicine or Endurance FEAT stops degradation. If Endurance reaches 0, the character suffers clinical death, with resuscitation possible for a number of rounds equal to their original Endurance rank number.
